## [Unreleased]

### Added

- The Working Reader, part 1 (#385, #387, #388; D-663, D-670 to D-673): the interface takes on its new design. A binder-cobalt rail on the left replaces the header and footer on every page: Library (with the number of documents), Open a document, Voices, and at its foot Settings and Privacy; a running job shows there under "Reading and recent" with how far it is. The interface speaks in Atkinson Hyperlegible and the text in Literata, both bundled with sci2audio (SIL Open Font License 1.1), so nothing is loaded from the internet. A new Settings page holds the appearance (as the system, light or dark), the interface language (moved here from the header), the default audiobook folder and speed of the voice, and "Announce the sentence being read to screen readers" (off; used by the coming Listen page). Light and dark colours, keyboard use and reduced motion are checked on every page.
- German voice (#211, VA-07): Kokoro Thorsten (`dm_thorsten`), the Kokoro model fine-tuned by Thorsten-Voice on the CC0 Thorsten-Voice dataset (Apache-2.0), reads German texts offline; pronunciation by espeak-ng, no text rewritten in the voice (D-467, D-470). The voice download comes in one pack per language (`voices download --language`), a job needs only its own language's pack, and a text is read by the chosen voice if it speaks the text's language, else by that language's default voice (D-468, D-469). The German weights are renamed on load and checked weight by weight, because kokoro 0.9.4 would otherwise skip some of them silently and speak noise.
- The text's language is guessed when the file states none (#219): sci2audio counts common German and English words over the whole text and reads it in German or English when one outnumbers the other at least 3 to 1 (at least 20 such words), so an untagged German PDF gets the German voice and the German rules without `--language de` (D-494, D-495). A file tagged English or German whose text is clearly the other language is read in the text's language, with a warning (D-496). Your language setting always wins. The report, the command line and the document page say where the language came from; a text too short or too mixed to tell is read in English with a warning (D-497 to D-500).
- A real German paper in the test corpus (#235): "PrivacyScore" by Maass, Laubach and Herrmann (arXiv 1705.08889, CC BY 4.0), with its hand-checked truth file, so German layout is tested on a real file (D-511, D-512). Reading it showed three layout gaps, now fixed: running heads of a text set in a small type area on bigger paper (the LNI template of German computer science) are found, because when nothing is printed in the paper's top tenth the first printed row counts as the page's top (D-502 to D-505); German captions ("Abb. 1:", "Tabelle 2:", "Tab.", "Algorithmus") name their figures and tables in any document (D-506); and footnote marks set as raised figures in the notes' own size start their notes (D-507).

- Notices in German (#212): in a German text the spoken notices and footnote introductions are German: "Tabelle 2 wird übersprungen.", "Abbildung 1 und Tabelle 2 werden übersprungen.", "Formel 4 wird übersprungen.", "Eine Abbildung wird übersprungen.", "Fußnote 1."; a short caption label such as "Abb. 3" is named in full. Footnote marks printed as raised figures in the text's own size, after a German closing quote, or as a "*" for a note marked "∗" are now left out of the running text too, so the German corpus paper reads none of its 30 marks aloud; English texts read exactly as before (D-517 to D-524). The document page has a "Language of the text" choice (automatic, English or German) in the check form: it applies to that document after "Update the analysis" and is not remembered for the next one; on the command line `--language` does the same.

- Footnotes are read where they are marked (#213): a footnote is read right after the sentence that holds its mark ("It works. Footnote 1. A note. It holds."), not at the foot of the column or the end of the document, so it comes while you still remember what it explains. A note marked in one chapter and printed in another is read, cut and counted in its mark's chapter, the report lists each move ("read after its mark (page 3)"), and the read-along player shows the note after the paragraph it is read in. A sentence ends where the voice would end it: "et al.", "Fig." or a German "3." end none. A note whose mark is not found, or whose mark stands only inside another note, is read where it is printed, as before; switching off "Footnotes" reads the marks and leaves the notes where printed, and "Footnote texts" still leaves all notes out (D-558).

- Subtitles (#214, OU-04): with "Also write subtitles (SRT and WebVTT)" on the document page, or `--subtitles` on the command line, sci2audio writes an `.srt` and a `.vtt` file next to each chapter MP3 and next to the M4B. They show the text as the voice speaks it, notices and spoken replacements included, on the read-along file's own times: a cue never runs across two sentences, holds at most two lines of 42 characters, and a long sentence is split after a ";", ":" or ",". The choice is off by default and remembered with the other settings; `--no-subtitles` switches a remembered choice off for one run (D-606). See `docs/subtitles.md`.
- Page progress and a quick cancel while a PDF is read (#343, #344): the job page shows "reading the file, page 120 of 400" and fills its progress bar, and the command line shows "Reading page 120 of 400" (one line per tenth of the book without a terminal, nothing with `-q`). Cancelling an audiobook job, or pressing Ctrl-C, while a PDF is read now stops its reader at once and frees its place for the next read; the job ends as cancelled, never as a damaged file. Each step's time limit stays exactly 60 seconds (D-623).
- Spelled-out abbreviations at your own pace (#295): the voice spells an all-capitals word it does not know, such as "NLP" or "LSTM", and the English voice did so at 6 to 7 letters a second, faster than its ordinary speech. A new setting "Spelled-out abbreviations" (Normal, Slower, Slowest; `--letter-pace` on the command line) slows only those letters, to about 4.5 and 3.3 letters a second in English; the rest of the sentence keeps its pace and the script stays as printed. Words the voice says as words ("BERT" in German, "NASA") are not slowed. Normal is the default and sounds as before (D-554).
- A speed control in the player (#152): the read-along player's bar has a Speed choice (0.75× to 2×) that works in every browser, keeps the voice's pitch, stays when the next chapter starts, after a jump and on the M4B, and the highlight follows at every speed. It is separate from the voice's own speed when the audiobook is made.
- The player remembers where you stopped (#153): the listening position is kept in the document's working folder, so the next time you start sci2audio the listen page offers "Continue from 3:12" or "Start from the beginning". It never starts playing by itself. Making the audiobook again forgets the position.
- Listen to an audiobook from the command line (#154): `sci2audio listen paper.pdf` opens what `sci2audio convert paper.pdf` made in the read-along player, with the text, in your browser. Name the folders as for `convert` (`-o`, `--work-folder`); the command checks the audiobook there first and says what is wrong ("the audiobook files are not in …", "made from another version of paper.pdf"). The interface only plays it: it serves that audiobook's files from those two folders and nothing else, and offers no document page for it.
- Choose the order of the chapters (#119): on the document page every chapter in the list has "Move up" and "Move down" buttons, and the audiobook reads the chosen chapters in the order of the list; on the command line `--chapters 3,1` reads chapter 3 first. When the order is not the printed one, the report and the listening script say so ("Read in the chosen order, not as printed: 3. Results, 1. Introduction."). The order is not remembered for the next document, as the choice of chapters is not.
- German abbreviations, symbols and units are read in full (#113): "z. B." reads "zum Beispiel", "S. 12" "Seite 12", "5 km" "5 Kilometer", "1 kg" "ein Kilogramm", from German word lists with case, gender and "u. a." before a year ("Müller und andere"); English reads as before.
- German numbers, dates and statistics are read as German says them (#130, #243): "2,45" reads "2 Komma 4 5", ranges read "bis", "am dritten Mai", "ein halb", "17 von 20", "3 zu 1", "13 Uhr 30", "10 hoch minus 4", "p kleiner als", "t mit 34 Freiheitsgraden"; a court file number such as "Az. 13 W 17/12" no longer reads "13 Watt".
- German links and citations are removed or read as German writes them (#101): "Die Daten sind unter https://x.org/d verfügbar." reads "Die Daten sind verfügbar.", an access note such as "(abgerufen am 12.03.2020)" goes with its link, and German author-year citations with "u. a.", "S." and "vgl." are left out as English ones are.
- German sentences end where German ends them (#227, #229): an ordinal or an abbreviation left as printed no longer cuts a sentence, and "Die Messung dauerte 2 Std. Danach …" is two sentences, as "2 Mio. Euro" stays one.
- More German readings (#230, #231, #233, #250, #264, #265, #358): a unit after "per", "pro" or "je" reads as a word ("pro Std." is "pro Stunde"), "f." after a page number reads "folgende", "p. a." reads "pro Jahr", and legal forms such as "i. S. v." and "i. V. m." are read in full instead of spelled.
- Code blocks are skipped with one notice (#280): "A code block is skipped." ("Ein Codeblock wird übersprungen."), instead of reading code line by line; the player shows the code under the notice, and the new category "Code blocks" switches it off. Code listings in PDFs and EPUBs are found as code blocks.
- Alphanumeric citation keys are read as citations (#254) when the reference list prints them as its entry labels: "[MH17]", "[Knu84]" or "[GKL+19]" are then left out as an aside, or read "reference MH17" where the sentence needs them. Without such a list, and for look-alikes such as "PM10", the bracket stays as printed. A reference list printed without a heading is left out too, when its entries count up from "[1]" to the end of the document (#92).
- PDFs are read in a separate process with limits (#185, #28): a damaged or hostile PDF can no longer take all memory or hang the app. The reader stops above 1 GB, each step (opening, each page, the outline) may take 60 seconds, and a page with more than 100,000 drawings and pictures, or a file with more than 500,000, is refused with a clear message. A reader that cannot start says so, and its start-up does not count against the first step (D-616).

### Changed

- Each document's audiobook goes into a folder of its own (#469, #552; D-749, D-750): in the audiobook folder you chose (by default Documents/sci2audio), a document's chapters, M4B, read-along and subtitle files go into a folder named after it, such as "paper"; a name already taken gets "paper (2)". sci2audio records in the document's `project.json` which files it wrote there, with their size and checksum, so it can later tell them from files you put there or changed. Its other data stays in sci2audio's own data folder; documents opened in an earlier version move there once, on the first start (#532).
- Removing a document from the library deletes its audiobook files too, after a warning (#470, #569; D-751, D-754): the box names the folder and how many files go, and "Keep the audiobook files" keeps them all. Only files sci2audio wrote into the document's own folder and that are unchanged since are deleted, and the folder goes only once nothing else is in it; a file you put there or changed, a link, and an audiobook made before each document had its own folder are never deleted, and the message after the removal says where files stayed.

- An EPUB's publisher pages are not chosen by default (#190): its imprint, colophon, copyright page, table of contents or index (tagged as such in the file) are left out of the chapters read, as the reference list is; you can choose them again. The title page, preface, chapters, epilogue and endnotes stay chosen. In The Time Machine, Imprint, Colophon and Uncopyright are left out. The document page's chapter list says why ("publisher's page, not read by default"), and the report JSON marks such a chapter with `"publisher": true` (#376).
- CI no longer runs when a label is added to a pull request: each label used to start a new run that cancelled the run of the same commit and left a red "CI passed" check on it (#360). The label `ci:full` now starts one run by hand on the PR's branch, on Linux, macOS and Windows, which no later run cancels. "CI passed" still fails whenever a job it needs was cancelled, skipped or failed (D-620).

### Fixed

- A PDF page with glyph-number text keeps its table, figure and formula notices (#574): on a page whose text comes partly from a font with no Unicode source, next to a captioned table or figure or a numbered formula, the step that reads "Table 2 is skipped." failed, and the document's notices were left out. They are read again.
- Turned PDF pages read as shown (#537, #543): on a page shown turned (`/Rotate`), text, pictures and drawings were placed on the unturned page, outside the page's own box; they now stand where the page shows them, so it reads as an upright page does. A crop box now also cuts off the sheet when the page takes its media box from the page tree, so a scanned spread reads only its own half on each page, and words of a scan's text layer skewed slightly either side of level stay on one line.
- A too-large PDF on a busy Windows machine gets the size message (#485): reading it could stop with a "BrokenPipeError" or "Windows Error 0xe0000008" instead of "reading it needs more than 1 GB of memory".
- Contents pages with their page numbers at the left edge, and page labels from running heads (#529, #535): a table of contents under "Contents", "Table of Contents", "Inhalt" or "Inhaltsverzeichnis" that prints each page number before its entry, as a journal issue does, is found and not read, as one with the numbers at the right edge is. A page number printed in a running head ("PrivacyScore 3") gives its page that printed page number in the report.
- Titles and lists in text files (#466): a short title line and the subtitle under it are two paragraphs, and lines opening with "- ", "* " or "• ", or a run of two or more numbered lines counting up ("1.", "2."), are list items, instead of all running into one sentence. A lone "1." line stays text, as a German date does.
- Resume after Pause goes on where the voice stopped (D-733): when the audiobook replaced an earlier one, Resume stopped at "The audiobook files exist already" and asked again, next to a button that freed the speech cache. Resume now keeps the run's own choice to replace, so it continues with the sentences already spoken. "Replace these files" says that the spoken sentences are kept and no longer offers to free the cache, and "Free the speech cache" now opens a confirm that says what it deletes before it deletes anything.
- The pieces of a long sentence sound like one sentence (#480): the voice can speak only so much at once, so a long sentence is cut at a clause and each piece is spoken alone. Each piece used to keep the voice's own silence before and after it, about 0.6 to 0.8 s at the cut. That silence is now left out at a cut and a short clause pause of 0.25 s takes its place, as at a comma; between whole sentences nothing changes. The read-along file marks a piece that goes on with the sentence before it (`"joins": true`, still version 1, so earlier versions read the file as before). Make an audiobook again to hear it; the spoken sentences are reused from the cache.
- A cut sentence is one sentence on the Listen page (#480): its pieces are marked as one, with one bar in the margin, also in Focus listening; Next and Previous sentence (and the arrow keys) step over it whole, Previous restarts it from its start, and a click plays it from its start. M sets one marker on the whole sentence, whichever piece is being read, and M again removes it; within 1.2 s of a later piece M marks this sentence, not the one before. The marker's tick on the timeline stands at the sentence's start. An audiobook made before this looks and steps as before.
- Citations read the same on a second pass (#305, #306, #308, #309, #291, D-577): a bracket next to or around one that is not read, or one whose link is removed, is now judged on the text without them, as a second pass over the script would. "Lee (Smith, 2019) (2019)" reads "Lee", "(Smith, 2019, https://example.org)" is not read, and "(see (Smith, 2019) (2019))" goes whole. A ";", "," or "and" left at a bracket's edge goes with what was removed, so "(; 2019)", "(see and Lee, 2019)" and "(x,)" are no longer heard (#272, #259). A citation glued between a full stop and a number stays as printed, so the two never join into a decimal. A nest of citations of any depth, such as "(see (see (Smith, 2019) (2019)) (2019))", is decided in one pass, and the work on one paragraph's brackets is capped at a fixed multiple of its length, so a deep nest cannot stall a job; a nest still undecided at the cap stays as printed (D-588, D-599).
- Footnote marks and German closing quotes read the same on a second pass (#315, #316, D-577): in German a sentence that ends in a closing quote ("Er sagte „ja.“ Dann ...") is cut there, so a note marked after it is read after that sentence, not at the end of the paragraph; the single guillemets ›…‹ and ‹…› count too. A footnote mark glued after an abbreviation, a unit or a number's full stop no longer hides it from the abbreviation, number, unit and statistics rules: "bzw.¹" reads "beziehungsweise", "z. B.¹" "zum Beispiel", "e.g.¹" "for example", "5 kg¹" "5 kilograms", and "Abschnitt 2.¹ Dann" no longer "2 Punkt". A mark inside a multi-part abbreviation ("z.¹ B.") still does (#341). With the "Footnotes" category switched off the marks stay and are read as before; text steps learn which categories are switched off from the new `StepContext.switched_off` field (D-595). English sentences are cut as before.
- Numbers read the same on a second pass (#304, #310, #318, D-577): a fraction after a minus sign ("−1/2") is read as a fraction on the first pass too, and "~" before a hyphen broken at a line end reads "about". In German, a date without its year before "1/2" or "1/3" is read ("am dritten April ein halb Tag"), and a range of two dotted dates where one or both have no year is read with "bis" when the first date is read: "3.4.-5.4.2019" is "dritten April bis fünften April 2019", "3.4.2019-5.4." is "dritten April 2019 bis fünften April". A day alone before the dash ("3.–5.4.2019") stays as printed.
- German dates, section numbers and caption labels read the same on a second pass (#334, #349, #249, D-605, D-615): a range of two dotted dates with a spaced en dash, an em dash or a spaced hyphen is read with "bis" and the second date in the dative, as the unspaced forms are: "3.4.2019 – 5.4." reads "dritten April 2019 bis fünften April". A number after the "Punkt" of a section number read aloud ("1 Punkt 2 Punkt 3. Tag") is no ordinal; after the word "Punkt" itself ("Der Punkt 3. Mai") it is, as before. A German notice names a figure or table as its caption reads the label's numbers: "Tabelle 2 Punkt 1 wird übersprungen.", "Abbildung 1 Punkt 2 bis 1 Punkt 4", "Abbildung 3 Komma 1", and a label the caption keeps as printed ("Tabelle 2.1a") stays so.
- Minus signs and powers read the same on a second pass (#336, #340, #210, D-605): a minus sign at a line end with its number on the next line reads "minus" ("−↵5" is "minus 5", "~−↵5" is "about minus 5"). A power whose exponent groups its thousands reads the whole exponent: "10^250 000 000" is "10 to the power of 250,000,000". A power of ten whose minus exponent runs into a caret power ("10−410^2") stays as printed.
- Units and signs read the same on a second pass (#320, #321, #335, D-605): an "x" after "equals", "less than or equal to" or "greater than or equal to" is the variable, as after "=", "≤" and "≥" ("Let f(x) = 4x." reads "equals 4x"); after a bare "less than" or "greater than" it still reads "times", as prose says "less than 2x slower". In German, "resp." joins labels as "bzw." does, so "1 resp. 2 V" reads "1 respektive 2 V", not "2 Volt". A unit after a fraction is left as printed on a second pass too ("3/4 s⁻¹", "17/20 kg").
- Links, citation nests and closing quotes read the same on a second pass (#326, #337, #338, #339, #278, #342, D-605, D-630): a link glued to the next bracket ends at its own closing bracket, so "(see https://x.org)(results)" keeps "(results)"; a join word, ";" or a spaced "/" left at a bracket's start after a removed link goes with it, and a bracket that stays after the link keeps the space before it ("Text (see https://x.org)(more)" reads "Text (more)"). A numeric citation after a removed citation or link is judged where the removal stood: "As shown in (see https://x.org) [12]" reads "As shown in reference 12", and "Ref. (Smith, 2019) [12]" reads "Reference 12". A year shell around a removed citation goes with it, so "((Smith, 2019) 2020) found it." reads "found it.", not "( 2020) found it.", and a nest that is a sentence's subject keeps its first citation's names. In German, an abbreviation before a closing quote keeps its full stop when the next word starts with a capital, so „usw.“ Dann reads „und so weiter.“ Dann and the sentence ends at the quote.
- Text, Markdown and EPUB files too large to read safely are refused with a clear message before they take gigabytes of memory (#174, #158): a text file of more than 500,000 lines, a Markdown file of more than 250,000 lines or 500,000 marks that can start markup (such as `*`, `_`, `` ` ``, `[`, `<`), and a book of more than 1,000,000 lines and pieces of text. Real books are far below these limits. Children of an element with many tags now share one copy of them, so such a chapter takes a tenth of the memory. An EPUB cover whose header asks for more than 10,000 pixels on a side or 25 megapixels is left out, as a cover over 20 MB already is, because decoding it could cost 2 GB in each run that opens it (#165) (D-579).
- For plugin authors: `sci2audio.testing.rebuilt_from_script`, which rebuilds a document from a first run's script for a second-run check, keeps a label a step rewrote, as the script reads it ("Abb. 1" spelled out becomes "Abbildung 1"), instead of dropping it; so a second run names the figure as the first did, and the second-run check now covers every corpus paper (#324, D-578). A label whose words were all removed still goes.
- A language tag as files often write it is read as the language it names (#237): "en_US" counts as en-US, "eng", "deu" and "ger" as en and de, and "und", "zxx", "mul", "mis" or a value that is no tag (such as "x-default") as no stated language, so the text's language is guessed. Before, "en_US" was taken as a language of its own and kept a German text from being read in German (D-513 to D-515).
- A grant or award number in brackets is not read (#285): a bracket that holds only codes with five digits or more, such as "(0801700)" or "(DMS-1912345)", goes with a marked edit instead of being read as a long number.
- The read-along shows a word joined at a line end without its hyphen (#286): "con-tributing" shows as "contributing", as the voice says it; a hyphen that stays, as in "COVID-19", shows as printed.
- An inline fraction reads its numerator and denominator apart (#202): a fraction bar drawn between them keeps the space PDFium gives, so "µB equals eℏ 2me" no longer runs them into one word.
- German texts read the same on a second pass (#253, #266, #269, #271, #303): rare numbers, an aside of decimal intervals, a decimal before a glued percent sign, a nested citation and a dotted date glued to a dash no longer read differently when the script is read again.
- A deep nest of brackets costs again what it did before the bracketed-identifier rule (#313).
- The job page's buttons wait for their click handlers (#177): "Replace these files", "Free the speech cache", "again" and "Cancel" are drawn disabled until the page's script runs, so an early click is never lost.

## [1.0.0] - 2026-11-14

The first release. sci2audio turns a scientific text (PDF, EPUB, Markdown or plain text, in English) into a faithful audiobook on your own computer: a listening script with every change marked and explained, MP3 files per chapter and an M4B with chapters, and a read-along player that highlights the original text. Everything runs offline after one voice download you start yourself.

### Added

#### Reading files

- PDF extraction (#23): records each page's text lines with font, size, bold, italic and position, its pictures and drawings, and the file's metadata as `Content`, deciding nothing (D-63, D-65); damaged, empty and password-protected files are refused with a clear message.
- Bold and italic from CM-Super and EC font names (#45), the T1 Computer Modern of most LaTeX papers, read from the cut in the name (`SFBX1095`, `SFTI1095`) as the cm-super font files define it.
- Outline in `Content` (#24, part 1): `Content.outline` keeps the file's own outline (PDF bookmarks, later an EPUB's table of contents) as `OutlineEntry`s with title as stored, level and target page, in the file's order, as a hint for headings (D-262 to D-266).
- PDF bookmarks (#24, part 2): PDF extraction records the file's bookmarks in `Content.outline`, in the file's order with their levels and target pages, titles exactly as stored; a GoTo action counts as a target, and a damaged outline gives what could be read without failing the file (D-266 to D-268).
- Content without positions (#73, part 1): `Page.order` keeps the stored order of lines and pictures where items have no boxes (EPUB, Markdown), `Picture.ref` where the file stores an image, and `OutlineEntry.item` the item an outline entry's anchor points at; all empty by default, so PDF content is unchanged (D-309 to D-311).
- EPUB extraction (#73, part 2, IN-02): each spine document becomes a page, each block element a text line with its markup as tags (element names, ids, `epub:type`, roles, language, links resolved to the item they point at), bold and italic from the HTML default rendering, pictures with their place, path and alternative text, the table of contents (navigation document or NCX) as the outline, and the package's title, authors, date, identifier and language, with the standard library only (D-304 to D-312). Damaged, encrypted, DRM-protected and oversized EPUBs are refused with a clear message (D-315). Cover images for the audiobook (OU-01): the cover an EPUB declares, or a PDF's first page as a PNG (D-313).
- Markdown and plain text (#74, IN-03): Markdown is read as CommonMark with tables, strikethrough, footnotes, dollar maths and front matter, through the same page reader as EPUB; a text file's encoding is guessed and `Source` names how its bytes were read (D-337 to D-352). Broken, oversized and binary files are refused with a clear message.
- Hostile and broken PDFs (#5, IN-01): golden files for the corpus papers and a set of broken files; scanned pages without a text layer, files cut short, locked files and files built to exhaust memory or time are refused with a message that says why, inside item and time limits (D-393 to D-404, D-419).

#### Layout: reading order, blocks and sections

- Layout (#21, IN-01): a new pipeline stage turns the extracted `Content` into a `Document` in reading order (D-63, D-67). Page furniture (running heads, page numbers, footers, line numbers, side stamps, a printed table of contents, a sidebar) is found and kept apart with its role, and each page's printed page number is kept as its label (#31). Columns are found band by band and read one after the other. Lines split by extraction are joined again, and paragraphs continue across columns and pages (#27).
- Block kinds and sections (#21): headings with their levels and printed numbers, and the section tree; footnotes with their marks, one block per note (#37); captions with their labels and list items with their markers; tables, figures and algorithms as placeholders their captions name (#38, #51); display formulas as placeholders with their printed numbers, a formula across both columns read once (#34).
- Outline as a heading hint (#24, part 3): layout uses the file's outline after the font rules: an entry adds a heading the font rules missed when exactly one paragraph on its page has its title as its whole text, and never removes a heading or changes its level; a single top entry naming the document is skipped (D-270 to D-272).
- A space between line pieces only where the page prints one (#36): a footnote mark, subscript or stacked script no longer reads as a word of its own.
- Running heads (#33): a body-size line at the top or bottom of two pages is a running head only when its exact text repeats on most pages, so "Algorithm 1" or a repeated table header stays in the text.
- Loose rules and marks (#58, #123, #163): a rule or small mark drawn inside a line stays in that line's block and no longer cuts its paragraph; a drawing on an algorithm caption's line stays with that caption.
- A heading with a rule drawn under it across the text width is a level-1 heading (#48).
- Documents without positions (#75, IN-02, IN-03): EPUB, Markdown and text are read from their tags (headings, paragraphs, lists, tables, figures, notes), not with the PDF rules; any EPUB navigation is furniture (D-405 to D-418, D-429). Blocks stay in page order after a paragraph is cut (#196).

#### Text rules: what the listener hears

- Text-step interface (#63, PE-10): text steps propose marked edits (remove, replace, pause, ask) with a reason, and the runner applies them in a fixed order; nothing rewrites text silently and nothing summarises (D-57). Every rule can be switched off by its category. The listening script is cut into utterances the voice can speak, keeping each one's place in the original text for read-along.
- Furniture, formulas, tables, figures, footnotes and the reference list (#64): page furniture is left out; formulas, tables and figures get a short spoken notice that names them; footnotes are read where their mark stands, as "Footnote 1." and its text; the reference list is left out by default.
- Characters and hyphenation (#65, IN-04): ligatures, mathematical letters and digits, soft hyphens and stray accents read as plain letters; a word hyphenated at a line end is joined again by one written rule, checked on every line-end hyphen of the corpus papers (TP-02).
- Citations, URLs, DOIs and e-mail addresses (#66): numbered and author-year citations, links, DOIs, arXiv numbers and e-mail addresses are left out with a marked edit, and the sentence around them still reads (D-167 to D-176). Footnote marks in EPUB and Markdown are tied to their notes by link tags (#189).
- Abbreviations, symbols and units (#68): read as words only where the voice would read them wrong ("e.g.", "Fig.", "mg/kg", "m²"), measured on the voice itself first (D-227 to D-243).
- Numbers, dates and statistics (#67): comparison operators, ± and minus signs, ranges, counts, powers of ten, test statistics with their degrees of freedom, R² and χ², confidence intervals and odds ratios read as the voice should; ordinary numbers, years and dates are left to the voice, which reads them well (D-176 to D-190).
- An abbreviation glued to a bracket no longer ends a sentence (#91).
- Reading rules sharpened in review (#109, #110, #145, #151, #159, #160): a URL cut at a line end no longer swallows an ordinary word on the next line ("and", "the"); a name with "the", "a" or "an" before it keeps its year in brackets ("the Olympics (2016)"); a bracket that holds only a citation and its lead words goes whole ("(See Smith (2019).)"); confidence intervals joined by "and", "or" or a comma are all read as intervals; units glued to a number or behind characters that are not spoken are read on the first run; an accent set apart from its letter and a size such as "5 x86K" read the same on every run.
- First German rules, ahead of v1.1 (#96, #221): German line-end hyphens (suspended hyphens before "und", "oder" and the like stay; split words are joined) and German reference-list titles ("Literatur", "Quellen", "Bibliographie" and others) when the job's language is German.

#### Voice

- Kokoro English voice (#18): the voice plugin contract and Kokoro plugin copied from Abogen (MIT, D-52) on PyTorch (D-48), 28 English voices with speed and a preview sample, 16-bit mono WAV files for the output stage, and one user-started download of everything needed offline (model, voices, spaCy English model), pinned by checksum and safe to interrupt (D-47, VA-03, VA-04). Conformance suite for voices in `sci2audio.testing`; nightly speech-recognition round trip and speed benchmark.
- Speed script and performance budget (#11, VA-02): `scripts/speed.py` measures the voice on the CPU and on the GPU (MPS), `docs/baseline.md` holds the budget, and the nightly run keeps the script working.

#### Audiobook

- Output (#69, OU-01, OU-03): one MP3 per chapter and one M4B with chapter marks, cover, title, authors, language and a label that says the voice is AI-generated; the read-along file ties each sentence to its place in the audio. ffmpeg comes with the `imageio-ffmpeg` package, with an ffmpeg on PATH as a fallback.
- The script job (#70, UC-01, UC-03): a file in, the listening script and a report of every change out, without a voice; chapters are the document's top-level sections, chosen by number. The report numbers chapters from 1, as the interface and the command line do (#104), and shows a line break inside a quoted edit as “↵” (#118).
- The audio job (#71, WI-02, VA-04): a file in, the audiobook out, entirely offline; an interrupted run resumes from its speech cache. The time left is estimated from a cost per spoken chunk fitted on the run (#136, D-380 to D-383).
- EPUB files in the app (#73, part 3, IN-02, OU-01): the script and audio jobs, the command line and the web interface read `.epub` files; the audio job writes the file's cover (an EPUB's declared cover, a PDF's first page) to the working folder and puts it into every MP3 and the M4B. A file without a usable cover, or a cover that cannot be written, gives an audiobook without one, never a failed job (D-313).
- Markdown files, text files and pasted text in the jobs, the command line and the interface (#74).

#### Command line

- Command line (#72, WI-03): `sci2audio script`, `convert` (resumes an interrupted run), `voices` with `voices download` (shows every file, size, licence and host and asks first, VA-03) and `clean`; chapters counted from 1, text-step categories switched with `--on`/`--off`, the settings file read as defaults, documented exit codes, Ctrl-C stops after the current sentence (D-210 to D-226). CI runs `sci2audio script` on a corpus PDF on Linux, macOS and Windows on every PR; a nightly test runs `sci2audio convert` with the real voice, killed and resumed (PE-02).

#### Interface and player

- Local server and job API (#76): `sci2audio ui` serves the interface on 127.0.0.1 only, at a port the system picks, behind a start-up token, with Host, Origin and Sec-Fetch-Site checks against other pages in the browser (D-192 to D-194). A JSON API under `/api/v1` opens files and pasted text, runs the pre-analysis, keeps the settings, lists the voices and starts the voice download only with consent to the exact download shown (D-197 to D-203), with progress as server-sent events. FastAPI's built-in OpenTelemetry is switched off (D-195). Tested with TestClient and Schemathesis (#7).
- The interface (#77, UC-01 to UC-03, AQ-01): a start page to open a file and see the documents opened before; a document page in three steps (check the analysis and its report, choose chapters and what is read, make the audiobook); the voice download with consent to the exact files shown; progress and a time estimate while it works; a job page with the result. Every page works with the keyboard alone and passes axe-core checks, in Chromium, Firefox and WebKit (#8, #205).
- Read-along player (#78, OU-02): a Listen page per document plays the audiobook chapter by chapter and shows the original text as printed, highlighting the sentence being read, scrolling with it until you scroll away, and playing from any sentence you click; skipped formulas, tables and figures show the notice the audiobook reads, printed page numbers mark where pages start, and a line names the synthetic voice. Every control is a button for the keyboard, nothing is announced while it plays, and the files are served only from the folder the audiobook was written to after checking they belong to it (D-294 to D-303).
- English and German interface and the privacy page (#79, AQ-02, AQ-03): every interface string in one table per language with the same keys (`web/strings/en.toml`, `de.toml`); the language comes from a switch on every page (remembered in a cookie), else the browser's language, else English (D-288 to D-292). Spoken words and the app's own messages stay English and are marked as such for screen readers (D-50, D-291). `/privacy` states what stays on the computer, with the download hosts and folders of the running server (D-293).
- Removing a document (#120, AQ-03, WI-02): the start page and each document's page offer “Remove this document”, a disclosure that says what goes (the copy of the file, its analysis and the speech cache, with their size) and what stays (the audiobook, in its folder, and the file you opened); the press of its button removes the document's whole working folder and nothing outside it. `DELETE /api/v1/documents/{id}` answers 409 while a job works on the document, here or in another process, and a removal that fails part-way names what is left and keeps the document listed for another try (D-353 to D-363).

#### Privacy and security

- Nothing leaves your computer: no account, no upload, no telemetry, no crash reports. The only network access is the voice download you start, pinned by checksum. The server binds to 127.0.0.1 only.
- OWASP ZAP baseline scan (#12, AQ-03): `scripts/zap_scan.py` starts the server with the test fakes, walks the main flow in Chromium through ZAP in English and German (open files, analysis, voice download, preview, audiobook, player, every API route, the 401 and 421 answers), lets ZAP crawl it, and fails on any High or Medium alert, on a Low one not accepted with a reason in `scripts/zap/accepted.toml`, and on any route or page language it never saw (D-364 to D-372). It runs in its own workflow on PRs that touch the server, nightly and by hand; the release checklist asks for its last run on `main`.
