## Must-fix

**M1. A table header row split into cells becomes a level-1 heading, because "longer than its words" is measured against the block's last line part only, not against the whole last row of text.**

Reproduction (made-up page, numbered paper, a table without a caption, booktabs rules from x 70 to 526, header cells "Model" at 76, "Accuracy" at 278, "F1" at 510 to 520, all bold 10 pt, rule 1 pt under them, rows below):

```
heading  ['c76_344', 'c278_344', 'c510_344', 't2']  'Model\nAccuracy\nF1'
```

The three cells join into one block of three "lines" (`_short` passes: 2 newlines, 3 words, bold), and the rule stands in it. `_ruled` takes the last part, "F1" (x 510 to 520), so the rule starts 44 em before it and passes `heading_rule_reach`, although it overhangs the row's words by only 6 pt (0.6 em) at each end. The same table with the header as one line is correctly not a heading. The same happens for a narrower table (x 170 to 370). This is a false heading on an ordinary construct, and it departs from D-385's "at least 1 em longer than its words": the words under which the rule stands are the whole row.

Fix: measure conditions 3 and 4 (and the vertical one) against the block's **last row**: the union of the boxes of the block's text lines that stand on the last line's height (for example, lines whose vertical middle lies inside the last line's box), not the last line alone. Add a unit test: an uncaptioned table whose bold header is split into two or three cells over a rule that overhangs them by 6 pt stays a paragraph. Corpus goldens must stay as they are now (the checklist titles are one line on their row); please confirm.
