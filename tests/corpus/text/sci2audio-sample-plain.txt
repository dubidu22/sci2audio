LISTENING TO PAPERS
A pilot note written for the sci2audio tests

This note describes a made-up pilot study and no real data. Every
sentence in it is our own, and its lines are wrapped by hand at about
seventy characters, as plain-text files often are.

1. Introduction

People listen to papers while they walk, cook or commute. Spoken text
runs at about 150 words per minute, so a 6,000-word paper takes forty
minutes. A word may be split across lines, as in this exam-
ple, which a listener hears whole.

	A paragraph that starts with a tab, followed by    several spaces.
   
The line above holds only spaces, so it ends a paragraph too. Some
readers prefer the café at noon; others read in Zürich at 7 °C. A dash
– en, and one — em; quotes “like these” and µs for microseconds.
